ARCHIVES, METHOD AND REPRODUCIBILITY


A more promising search pool
Kepler Bonus supplied this lead. Its long light curves include hundreds of thousands of background
sources outside the original Kepler target list. Our catalog cuts left 1,800 background M dwarfs; the
64 brightest eligible stars were actually searched in this pilot.

Archive ranking is a scientific judgment, not a quantified probability of discovery. Cluster stars often have
stronger variability; alternate TESS extractions reuse the same photons. More data are useful only when
quality, contamination and prior searches are accounted for.

Validation of the workflow
Known controls recovered LHS 3844 b and Kepler-138 c/d, explicitly excluded from discovery claims.
Synthetic tests checked transit recovery and holdout separation. The initial TESS screen yielded nine signals
and five false-screen passes in 100 inverted runs, so screen scores alone were never treated as planets.

The archive review found QLP timing errors in sectors 99-104 affecting 44 selected stars. All 88 affected
two-band runs were repeated with those QLP products removed. Original results are retained as superseded
evidence. Revised holdouts are retrospective; earlier conclusions were corrected.

The Kepler pilot reserved the last 25% of usable quarters, rounded up, before searching. Its second initial
survivor has secondary dimming and weak aperture support. None of 64 inverted controls passed the first
screen. These counts do not provide a formal false-alarm probability.
